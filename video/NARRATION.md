# SightLine demo video: narration

The voiceover for the 3-minute Devpost video. Each numbered clip below becomes one mp3 in `video/voiceover/`, so you can drop it into the editor and slide it to match the footage. The gaps between clips are where the app speaks its own answers. The app and the narrator are both Matilda, so SightLine sounds like one voice throughout.

Regenerate after editing any line:

```bash
npm run voiceover
```

Only the lines starting with `>` are spoken. Write numbers as words so the voice says them the way you want. Timings are targets, not hard cuts.

### 01 hook (0:00–0:18)
Shot: black screen, the stat in large type, then the SightLine logo.
> At least two point two billion people live with a vision impairment. But menus, medicine labels and street signs still assume you can see them, and that you can read the local language.
> Meet SightLine. Point your camera. Hear the world.

### 02 point (0:18–0:35)
Shot: a hand points the iPhone at a medicine box. The tracking brackets lock on, and the Detect card names the product. Tap the shutter.
> Here's a box of pain reliever. I just point my phone. SightLine spots what I'm holding, and one tap reads what matters: the dose, the warnings, and the expiry date.

App speaks its answer here (about 10 seconds).

### 03 web (0:45–1:00)
Shot: the "From the web" card slides up with sources and a product photo.
> Then it checks the web. With Bright Data, SightLine searches for that exact product, and any recall or F.D.A. safety warning always comes first, with the sources on screen.

### 04 barcode (1:00–1:12)
Shot: turn a snack or drink to its barcode. The barcode card appears on its own.
> Found a barcode? SightLine reads it automatically and pulls up the exact product: ingredients, allergens and nutrition.

### 05 translate (1:12–1:25)
Shot: point at the Spanish caution sign in Translate.
> Traveling? Point it at a sign in Spanish, and SightLine translates it into your language, in a natural voice. It speaks thirteen languages.

App speaks the translation here (about 5 seconds).

### 06 ask (1:32–1:45)
Shot: tap Ask, speak the question, the answer appears.
> And it remembers. Ask, "was that medicine I scanned earlier recalled?" An agent searches your scan history and answers in a couple of seconds.

App speaks its answer here (about 5 seconds).

### 07 hat (1:52–2:10)
Shot: put on the cap with the camera. Look at a label, press the hat's button, and the phone in your pocket speaks.
> Need your hands free? This is a fifteen-dollar camera on a baseball cap. It watches what's in front of you, reads it as soon as it's steady, or when you press the button, and your phone speaks the answer.

App speaks its answer here (about 6 seconds).

### 08 tech (2:18–2:45)
Shot: the architecture diagram, or logos one by one: Claude, ElevenLabs, Bright Data, Neon.
> Under the hood, Claude reads every photo and returns structured answers. ElevenLabs gives SightLine its voice, with the first audio in about two hundred milliseconds, and Scribe turns voice commands into text. Bright Data brings in live web results. And Neon Postgres holds the cache, your scan history and the agent's memory, so a repeat scan comes back in a fraction of a second.

### 09 close (2:45–3:00)
Shot: the phone, the cap, then the logo and the live link.
> SightLine works on any phone, as a web app with nothing to install or as an iPhone app. Built for people who can't see it, and for people who can't read it.
> SightLine. Point your camera. Hear the world.
